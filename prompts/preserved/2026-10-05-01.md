## Why

The owner (RECORD -519): "removing reliance on network data, but not total removal of collection at all. My goal is that for each failure, there is another method available that can help fill the gaps." Today that does not hold for live fixes. -510 found (RECORD -521) that the live gate tests accuracy alone (`domain/LiveFixGate.kt:71-74`), so a network fix of 50 m or better becomes the gated fix that "Arrived", the waypoint's line and a new find's location read. The S22 delivered network fixes as good as 15 m at the desk. The provider is known when each fix arrives and is dropped there: `location/AndroidLocationTracker.kt` `toFix()` (about `:89-97`) and `LocationFix.Update` (`domain/LocationTracker.kt:45-53`) has no field for it. The only discriminator after that point is the timestamp rule (`domain/NetworkProviderFix.kt:48`).

This is the first half of what was called the quick win. The owner split it (RECORD -526): judging GPS fixes by satellite status waits until the walk logger shows it is needed. **No satellite status, no GnssStatus callback, no new accuracy threshold in this dispatch.**

## The behaviour

1. Every live fix carries the provider it came from: GPS, network, or an explicit unknown (a null provider, "fused", "passive", anything else). Never a guess.
2. **Nothing that acts on position uses a network or unknown fix.** It may still be shown: on the map it is -510's approximate position, whatever its accuracy. A 15 m network fix shows as approximate, not precise.
3. GPS fixes keep the existing 50 m gate.
4. The timestamp rule stays where there is no provider: stored track points (no schema change in this dispatch) and anything built from them.
