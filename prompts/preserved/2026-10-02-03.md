# Rulings on the coder's findings (continuation 2026-09-28-418)

Written by the planner on 2026-10-02 (UTC), after the coder's report-first message, read at `main` `c42d857e` on branch `way-back-route`. Given to the coder by message first; this is the same text for the record.

## What the coder found (read, not run)

- **`PathHome` does not project the walker onto the path.** It places them at the track's most recent stored point, by an owner ruling against nearest-point projection (`domain/PathHome.kt:15-24`). The hop is the straight line from the walker to that point; the hop bands are NONE, COUNTED above 25 m (left below 20), FAR above 50 m (left below 45).
- **The route's distance** runs through the track joined to itself wherever it passes within 10 m of itself (`domain/TrackSelfJoin.kt:33`), plus the counted hop, plus the origin leg. The graph code returns metres only, not the route's points.
- **"Off the route" as HopBand.FAR** is the only line in the code, and it measures distance to the most recent stored point, not to the path. Because the track goes on recording during the return, a walker who strays has the stray recorded, and it becomes part of the route home.
- **The route and the off-track alert can disagree in two ways a user would see:** following a route that bends away from the start can raise "Off track" while the HUD shows the route; a walker far from the last stored point can get "Unable to calculate route" with no alert. Not reconciled here; the alert's meaning is plan task T21.
- **The stored track lags the walker** by 30 to 45 s (the service writes every 30 s or 20 kept points; the screen reads every 15 s), about 27 to 40 m at the default walking pace, which is more than the 25 m lookahead.
- A correction to the dispatch: `pathHome` has one production caller; `ReturnWalkingTime` has none.

## The rulings

1. **The lookahead is measured (hop + 25 m) along the route from the most recent stored point** (the coder's option B). It stays along the walked route, uses no nearest-point projection and adds no number. The case where it can still land behind the walker (a path winding hard inside the lag) is held by a test that shows what happens. **Not chosen:** 25 m from the stored point (points backwards often); nearest-point projection for aiming (against an owner ruling, so the owner's to choose, and put to them only if the walk shows this is not good enough); a faster flush in the service (its batching stays off limits; reported as a finding if the coder thinks the lag is the real fault).
2. **The corner guard:** the lookahead stops at the last route point for which every route point before it lies within the self-join distance (10 m) of the straight line from the walker. A reuse of the self-join ruling in a second role, said so in the code, and provisional with the 25 m.
3. **The graph code returns the route's points,** with `joinedTrackHome` a thin wrapper whose behaviour is unchanged; its tests and `PathHome`'s pass unedited. No second copy of the graph code.
4. **The shape as proposed:** `domain/RouteHome.kt`, answering either a point ahead with the same total distance `PathHome` gives, or withheld because off the route or because there are no usable points. A track shorter than the lookahead aims at the route's end. The 5 s cadence is described for T6 and not wired.

# A correction to ruling 2: the corner guard is read from the walker's assumed place (continuation 2026-09-28-419)

Written by the planner on 2026-10-03 (UTC), after the coder stopped while writing the tests. Given to the coder by message first; this is the same text for the record.

**What the coder found.** Ruling 2 said the lookahead stops at the last route point for which every route point *before it* lies within 10 m of the straight line from the walker. Ruling 1 places the walker `hop` metres along the route past the most recent stored point P. The route points between P and that place are behind the walker, and their distance from the walker's line is up to `hop`. So once the hop passes 10 m the guard stops at P, and the needle points back at the stored point the walker has already walked on from: exactly the lag case ruling 1 exists for. Worked example from the coder: an out-and-back with the walker 22 m past P; ruling 1 gives a point 25 m ahead of the walker, and the guard as worded pulls it back to P, 22 m behind.

**The correction (the coder's option A).** The guard checks only the route points from `hop` metres along the route onward, up to the lookahead. The planner's wording in ruling 2 was wrong; this replaces it.

**Its cost, stated and tested, not softened:** when the walker is beside the route rather than along it (30 m off in the coder's test), the points in the first `hop` metres are not checked, and the walker is pointed about 45 m up the path from a spot 30 m beside it. Held by a test that states this as what the rule does, and named in the report beside the case where the lookahead can land behind the walker. Both are for a walk to judge.

**Not chosen:** the guard as worded (it undoes ruling 1 whenever the hop passes 10 m); no guard (the decision record names aiming across a bend as the failure that costs something on a forest path, and with the lookahead at hop + 25 m the route segment can be longer than 25 m).
