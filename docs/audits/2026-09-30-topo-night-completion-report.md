**Choosing the treatment and the change-over.**
- *Half-amplitude V1.* Scaling V1's output (`min` 0.5) puts the mean at 0.208-0.233 and the ground at 0.196-0.239 on tile zoom 7-10: inside both bands. The scale was picked to centre both at once; matching the ground alone would want about 0.4 (mean 0.17, below the band), matching the mean alone about 0.6 (ground 0.26+, above it). 0.5 is a judgement inside that range, not a fit.
- *A step at 9.5, not a ramp.* The content jump is itself at 9.5 (tile level 10 to 11), so the paint changes where the tiles do. A ramp ending at 9.5 would bring the last half zoom of the old tiles back to V1, which is the light state being fixed; a ramp running past 9.5 would darken tiles that already look right, which the dispatch said not to touch.
- *How the flat-grey crossing is avoided.* The shader computes `min + (max - min) * rgb`; blending from an inverted paint (`min` > `max`) to a non-inverted one passes through `min = max`, where every pixel is the same grey. Both states here are inverted (`min` 0.5 and 1, `max` 0 throughout), so the slope `max - min` never reaches zero and no value in between, at any zoom, can do it. The same structure is why no ramp was needed to hide it.
- *Why no paint can make roads and labels both lighter.* The spin preserves each pixel's channel mean, and saturation/contrast/brightness are affine, so the output's channel mean is an affine function of the input's. At low zoom black labels (mean 0) < ground (0.4-0.6) < pale roads (about 0.76) in day, so an inversion puts labels above and roads below the ground, and no inversion the reverse. Proof by the tile data, not the SDK; it does not cover a perceptual-luminance argument across hues, which was not attempted.

## The model and its limits
`RasterShaderModel.kt` (beside `NightColour.kt`): `rasterShade` models `shaders/raster.fragment.glsl` and `raster_layer_tweaker.cpp` at 13.5.0 (read from the tag for this change: spin weights, saturation toward the un-spun pixel mean, contrast about 0.5, `mix(min, max, rgb)`, clamp, round to 8 bits). `rasterPaintAt` evaluates a paint object at a zoom, with numbers, `["step", ["zoom"], ...]` and `["interpolate", ["linear"], ["zoom"], ...]`; any other expression throws (no guessed value). `rasterTileZoomFor` is the tile-level rule. Check against today's V1: `rasterShade` with V1 equals `nightColorOf` within 1 level on every distinct colour of all 81 tiles (test). **Where it may differ on the device:** no bilinear resampling between tile pixels at fractional zoom; no parent/child tile cross-fade (`raster-fade-duration`, where two tile levels are drawn under one paint at once, including the moment the paint steps while the old level is still on screen); no premultiplied-alpha edges; no `raster-*-transition` easing of a paint change; GPU rounding. The model's V1 was measured against S22 captures at mean |error| 0.63/255 (swatch board, section 2); the zoom-dependent paint has **not** been measured on a device.
